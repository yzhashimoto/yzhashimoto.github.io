I am a criminologist and social psychologist. My research examines pro- and anti-social behaviour in its social and cultural context, and its implications for policy and practice, with a focus on cross-national comparison.

I am an SNSF Research Fellow at the University of Fribourg, where I work on PREVICT-SW, a participatory project to prevent violence against sex workers, and a Hearing Officer at the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). My doctoral thesis at the University of Lausanne examines national culture and cross-national differences in homicide.

Before this, I spent more than five years at the School of Criminal Justice of the University of Lausanne, working on the Council of Europe Annual Penal Statistics (SPACE) and the European Sourcebook of Crime and Criminal Justice Statistics. My methodological expertise lies in survey design and administration, and in quantitative analysis.

Research

Culture and violence

A comparative culturological study of cross-national differences in homicide (PhD project).

Violence against sex workers

PREVICT-SW, a quasi-experimental participatory study on victimisation and its prevention, University of Fribourg.

European crime and penal statistics

SPACE, the Council of Europe Annual Penal Statistics on prisons and probation, the European Sourcebook of Crime and Criminal Justice Statistics, and the EU-funded LINCS project.

Victimisation and vulnerable groups

Victimisation of Roma, homelessness during the COVID-19 pandemic, femicide, and child sexual abuse in the Catholic Church.

Religion, ageing and volunteering

Religious practice, well-being and volunteering among older people in Switzerland, with the Institute for Social Sciences of Religions, University of Lausanne.

Survey methodology

Questionnaire design and response rates, including survey tools for Swiss police forces and online teaching evaluations.
