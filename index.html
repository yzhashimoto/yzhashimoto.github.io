I study anti-social behaviour in its social and cultural context, and what it means for policy and practice. My work is comparative: across countries, across criminal justice systems, and across cultures.

I am currently an SNSF Research Fellow at the University of Fribourg, working on PREVICT-SW, a participatory project on preventing violence against sex workers. I am also a Hearing Officer at the State Secretariat for Migration, where I conduct hearings and draft decisions on asylum applications.

I lean towards quantitative methods and survey design, and I also work with qualitative data. My own background is Brazilian, Italian, Japanese and Swiss, which shapes how I approach comparisons.

Research

Violence against sex workers

PREVICT-SW, a quasi-experimental participatory study on victimisation and its prevention, University of Fribourg.

European penal statistics

SPACE, the Council of Europe Annual Penal Statistics on prisons and probation, and the European Sourcebook of Crime and Criminal Justice Statistics.

Religion and well-being

Religious practice, self-identification and well-being among older people in Switzerland, with the Institute for Social Sciences of Religions, University of Lausanne.

Survey methodology

Questionnaire design and response rates, including online evaluations in higher education.
